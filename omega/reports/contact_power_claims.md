What the sessions contain:
- **Jan 1987, "ET bases" (training):** the viewer reports bases on Titan, Mars and Earth, and writes
  of beings he was "unable to make contact with". No coordinates on Titan, so nothing to check.
- **22 May 1984, "Mars, 1 million BC":** coordinates 40.89°N 9.55°W read out by a monitor in the room;
  the transcript does not show the monitor was blind to the target. The viewer reports a pyramid,
  "megalithic" structures and "very tall … thin" people. The first coordinate is in Cydonia, about
  9 km from the "Face" mesa, which later high-resolution imaging shows to be a natural landform

operations", and "continued use of remote viewing in intelligence gathering operations is not

Hyman disagreed. That dispute is about lab statistics. On operational information it was unanimous.

## 7. Test 6: a "contact signature" in sighting data (task 4.2)

**Hypothesis.** Maintained contact at fixed sites should show up as reports that recur more
*regularly* than chance, or with a site-specific *period* the national reporting profile lacks.
Ordinary reporting is bursty (flaps, media, launches, fireworks).

**Method** (`omega/contact_signature.py`). 383 NUFORC places with ≥ 40 distinct report-days,
1995–2023. Local regularity CV2 (1 = Poisson, < 1 regular, > 1 bursty) and maximum Rayleigh power over
periods of 2–400 days. Null: the same number of days drawn from the national report-day profile
(which keeps the weekly cycle, July 4, seasons and the 2010s boom); 200 draws per place, 4,000 more for
any place with p ≤ 0.01; Benjamini–Hochberg across places.

**Result.**
- **Regularity: 0 of 383 places** are more regular than the national profile (median CV2 1.035).
- **Site-specific period: 6 places, all annual** (360–366 days): Ocean City MD, North Myrtle Beach SC,
  Myrtle Beach SC, Toronto, Indianapolis, Chicago. Their peaks fall from 17 July to 2 September. Ocean
  City has 66% of its report-days in June–August against a median of 28% elsewhere. Annual strength
  rises with latitude across all 383 places (Spearman ρ = 0.29, p = 8 × 10⁻⁹). These are beach-season
  crowds and long northern summer evenings, not schedules (`contact_signature_annual.py`).

No contact signature. The test can be re-run on any candidate site list, including treatment sites
if the owner's contact protocol (4.1) is ever run.

## 10. Test 9: the Atomic Energy Act mechanism (UAP Disclosure Act finding 4)

Finding (4) of SA 2610 says UAP records escape declassification "due in part to exemptions under the
Atomic Energy Act of 1954", and through an over-broad reading of "transclassified foreign nuclear
information". In the 286 PURSUE records:

- The **2015 Pantex unidentified-object incident report** (DOE-UAP-D005) and its image (D001) carry
  UCNI markings under Section 148 of the AEA (42 USC 2168). The mechanism is real for at least this
  record. It was released anyway.
- No Restricted Data, Formerly Restricted Data or TFNI marking on any record (one bibliography line
  cites a 1955 "Top Secret/Restricted Data" report).
- 21 records carry (b)(3) redactions. Where the statute is printed (6 records) it is 10 USC 424 (4),
  10 USC 130b (1) or 50 USC 3024(i) (1). None cite the AEA.

Survivor bias limits this: records withheld whole under the AEA would not be in a release at all. A
FOIA or MDR denial letter citing 42 USC 2162 for a UAP record would settle it.
